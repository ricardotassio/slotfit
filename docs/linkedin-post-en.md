# LinkedIn draft — English

A solver doubled recovered time in my demo—and I still rejected the strategy.

That tension is exactly why I built SlotFit.

After ten years around healthcare software, I wanted to explore a familiar
operational problem without adding another chatbot: when a cancellation creates
two openings, can simple, explainable software place compatible requests more
effectively?

In a controlled synthetic fixture, FIFO and local best-fit each recover 60 of 120
minutes. A small CP-SAT constraint model sees the global conflict and recovers all
120, with three optimal lexicographic phases.

But fixture optimality is not enough. Across 40 synthetic development seeds, I
tested four longitudinal policies and kept utilization separate from repeated
exclusion. The closest candidate degraded the worst supported slice by 10.80
percentage points. My predeclared limit was 10.00.

So I stopped. No threshold adjustment. No calibration. No final held-out run.

The repository also includes:

- deterministic synthetic scenarios and stable reason codes;
- opaque-ID and input-order counterfactual tests;
- a framework-independent domain with FastAPI and Vue adapters;
- an append-only, idempotent simulated-review log;
- an accessible calendar/table comparison and explicit human boundary.

This POC uses synthetic data and performs no booking, patient contact or clinical
prioritization. It does not claim revenue, access, wait-time or patient-safety
improvement. What it demonstrates is the part I care about most: choosing the
simplest useful technology, measuring the uncomfortable trade-off and preserving
a no-go when the evidence says no.
